Surakarta Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Surakarta - useful if you're rusty, or learning. The looping-track capture is the one part almost everyone finds confusing at first, so there's a full worked example below.

The basic idea

The board
A plain 6x6 grid of points. Each player starts with 12 stones filling the two rows nearest their own side (Black on the top two rows, White on the bottom two), with the middle two rows empty. Black moves first.
Moving
On your turn, slide one stone a single step to any adjacent empty point - straight or diagonal, any of the 8 directions. A step like this is always quiet: it never captures anything, even if it lands right next to an enemy stone.
The loop tracks
Four lines on the board - the 2nd and 5th row, and the 2nd and 5th column - don't just stop at the edge: each end of each of these four lines curves outward and connects to the nearest end of the neighboring special line at that same corner, forming a loop around each of the board's 4 corners. A stone sliding along one of these four lines can ride a loop off one edge and reappear travelling along the connected line, still moving in a straight line as far as the track and any further loops allow.
Capturing
Capturing is completely separate from moving, and only works along the loop tracks. Instead of taking a single step, a stone can slide all the way along one of its 8 directions - passing straight over every other stone, friend or foe, without effect - for as long as the line and its loop connections carry it. The moment it has gone around at least one loop AND the next stone it reaches is an enemy stone, that stone is captured and the slider lands on its square, ending the move right there. If the line runs off the board with no loop to continue it, or if it loops all the way around without ever meeting a qualifying enemy stone, there's no capture that way. Capturing is optional here: even with a capture available, you may play a quiet step instead.

○ is White (the piece that moves in this example), ● is Black. White's stone at A sits on one of the four loop-carrying lines. There's nothing blocking it all the way to the edge, so instead of a quiet step, White plays a full capturing slide.

How a game ends

Winning
You win by capturing every one of your opponent's stones, or if your opponent has no legal move at all on their turn.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
