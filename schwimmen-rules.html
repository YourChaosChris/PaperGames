Thirty-One Rules

How PaperGames plays Thirty-One, known in German-speaking countries as Schwimmen or Schnauz.

The basic idea

Cards and players
A pack of 32 cards (Seven to Ace in four suits), 2 to 6 players. Everyone gets three cards, and three more are laid face up in the middle.
Counting a hand
Sevens to Tens count their pips, Jack, Queen and King 10 each, the Ace 11. A hand counts only the cards of ONE suit - the best suit you hold. The most you can have is 31: an Ace and two ten-point cards of the same suit. Three cards of the same rank, such as three Kings, count 30.5; three Aces count 31. Your own score is always shown under your cards.
Your turn
Do exactly one thing: swap one of your cards with a middle card, swap all three of your cards with the middle, pass, or knock. If every player passes in a row, the middle cards are put aside and replaced by three new ones from the stock.
Knocking
Knock when you think your hand is good enough. Every other player then gets exactly one more turn, and all hands are shown. Only one player can knock per round.
Thirty-one
Whoever reaches exactly 31 - also with the cards dealt - shows at once, and every other player loses a life.
Lives and swimming
Everyone starts with three lives, shown as filled circles. After the cards are shown, the lowest score loses a life; on a tie for lowest, all of them lose one. A player without lives is 'swimming' and still plays; losing once more means out. The last player left wins. If the last players go out together, the game is a draw.
The version played here
Thirty-One is played with many house rules. Here knocking is allowed from the very first turn, and if the middle has to be replaced but fewer than three cards are left in the stock, the round ends with a showdown straight away.
