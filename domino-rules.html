Domino Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays the classic draw game with a double-six set - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The tiles
A double-six set has 28 tiles. Each tile has two halves showing 0 to 6 pips, and every combination appears exactly once - from 0-0 to 6-6. A tile with the same number on both halves is a double.
Dealing
With two players each gets 7 tiles, with three or four players each gets 5. The remaining tiles stay face down as the stock.
Who starts
Whoever holds the highest double opens by playing it. If nobody has a double, the highest tile opens (the most pips, then the higher half). PaperGames plays this opening tile automatically.
Playing a tile
On your turn, add one tile to either open end of the line. The touching halves must show the same number, so the tile's other half becomes the new open end.
Drawing and passing
If none of your tiles fits, you draw from the stock until you get one that does. Once the stock is empty and nothing fits, you pass.

How a round ends

Going out
The first player to play their last tile wins.
A blocked line
If every player has to pass in a row, the line is blocked. Then everyone adds up the pips left in their hand: the lowest total wins, and a shared lowest total is a draw. The totals of all players are shown at the end.
One round
A game here is a single round - there's no running score over several rounds, the same way backgammon is handled in this collection.

Playing on the screen

Ends, line and hand
The two open ends are shown at the top as big numbers - they are what you tap to place a tile. Below them, the line played so far runs as one row you can scroll sideways. Your own tiles are at the bottom: tap one to pick it, then tap the end it should go on. Tiles that don't fit sit a little lower with a dashed outline.
Several people, one device
When you play with others on the same device, your tiles are covered after your turn. Hand the device on, and the next player taps "Show my tiles" to see only their own.
