Futoshiki Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Futoshiki, the Japanese inequality puzzle.

The basic idea

The grid
The grid has 4 x 4, 5 x 5 or 6 x 6 cells. Fill it with the numbers 1 up to the grid size, so that each of them appears exactly once in every row and exactly once in every column.
The signs
Between some neighbouring cells there is a small triangle. It works like a less-than sign: its narrow tip points to the cell with the smaller number, its wide end to the cell with the larger one. Every sign must hold in the finished grid.
Given numbers
Some puzzles start with a few numbers already filled in, shown in bold; many have none at all. Given numbers can't be changed.
One solution
Every puzzle is freshly generated and checked to have exactly one solution, which can be found step by step without guessing.

Playing

Entering numbers
Select a cell, then pick a number below the grid. Erase clears the selected cell, Undo takes back your last entry. A number that repeats in its row or column, or two numbers that break a sign, are shown inverted.
Hint
Hint shows the next step in three presses: the first marks a cell, the second gives the reason, the third enters the number. It never guesses. A win with a hint counts separately in the statistics.
Daily Challenge and printing
The Daily Challenge is a 5 x 5 puzzle that is the same for everyone on the same day. Print puts the empty puzzle on one A4 page, to solve on paper.

Finishing a puzzle

Solved
The puzzle is solved as soon as every cell is filled, no number repeats in a row or column, and every sign holds.
