Tablut Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Tablut, the 9x9 member of the Tafl family - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The setup
The king starts on the central throne in a cross of eight defenders, besieged by eight attackers stationed two per edge. Attackers move first, and the two sides chase very different goals: the king's side wants him to reach a corner, the attackers want to trap him first.
Moving
Every piece - attacker, defender, or the king - moves like a rook: any distance in a straight line, but never through or onto another piece.
The throne and corners
The center throne and the four corner squares are off-limits to everyone but the king: attackers and defenders can neither land on them nor pass through them, though the king may freely re-enter the empty throne. The corners are the king's only escape squares.
Capturing
A regular piece (attacker or defender) is captured by custodian capture: trap it between two enemy pieces, or one enemy piece and a hostile special square (the empty throne, or a corner), on opposite sides of a straight line. Only the piece that just moved can trigger a capture, so moving a piece between two enemies yourself is always safe.

Worked example

Two short examples of how the sandwich works in practice (using the classic a-i / 1-9 board coordinates, column then row):

An ordinary sandwich
A Swedish defender at d6 has a Muscovite attacker sitting at c6, immediately to its west. When a second attacker slides into e6, the defender is now trapped between two attackers in a straight line - and is captured.
A throne-assisted sandwich
A defender at e6 sits just south of the empty throne (e5). A single attacker sliding into e7, immediately south of the defender, is enough to capture it: the empty throne counts as hostile on the opposite side, so no second attacker piece is needed.

Winning the game

Capturing the king
The king is captured only when all four squares orthogonally adjacent to him are hostile: occupied by attackers, or - for a side facing the throne - the empty throne itself, which counts as an attacker-equivalent hostile square. Unlike a regular piece, the board edge never counts as hostile to the king in this ruleset: a king pushed to the rim of the board still needs a real attacker on every open side.
How the game ends
The king's side wins the instant he reaches any of the four corners. The attackers win by capturing the king as described above. A side with no legal move at all on its turn loses.
