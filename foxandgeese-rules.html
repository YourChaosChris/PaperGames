Fox and Geese Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Fox and Geese, the medieval hunt game of one fox against a flock of geese.

The basic idea

The board
The board is a cross of 33 points: a 7x7 grid without its four 2x2 corners, the same shape as the English Peg Solitaire board. Points are joined across and down; in the version played here there are no diagonal lines.
Setting up
The fox (a triangle) starts on the centre point. The 13 geese (filled discs) fill the bottom arm of the cross and the whole row above it. The geese move first.
Moving
On your turn, move one piece one step along a line to an empty neighbouring point. The fox may move in all four directions. A goose may move forward (up) or sideways, but never back.
Capturing
The fox captures by jumping over a neighbouring goose to the empty point directly behind it; the goose is removed. With the same move it may jump again, also in a new direction, as often as it can. Capturing is never compulsory, and the fox may stop after any jump. The geese cannot capture or jump.

How a game ends

Winning
The geese win when the fox cannot move on its turn. The fox wins as soon as only 2 geese are left, or if the geese cannot move on their turn.
Resignation
Either side can resign at any time. There is no automatic draw: if neither side can make progress, end the game by resigning or start a new one.

Which version is played here

Fox and Geese has been played in many versions: with 13, 15, 17 or more geese, with diagonal lines on the board, with geese that may move backwards, and with compulsory capture. PaperGames plays the old form with 13 geese on a board without diagonals: the geese move first and never back, and the fox may jump several times in one move but never has to capture. A goose may only move forward (towards the far side of the cross) or sideways; versions that allow a diagonal step forward don't apply on a board without diagonals.
