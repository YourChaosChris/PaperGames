Game History Library

Every game in this collection has its own story - some over five thousand years old, some barely a decade. Read through them here, in roughly the order each game first appeared, or jump straight to any one's full history page.

Dates for the oldest games are approximate - their exact origins are often lost to history or debated by historians.

  1. c. 3100 BCE

    Senet

    One of the oldest board games ever found - and one whose exact rules have been lost for thousands of years.

    Full history →
  2. c. 2600 BCE

    Ur

    At roughly 4,600 years old, the Royal Game of Ur is one of the oldest board games ever discovered - and, thanks to a piece of detective work spanning two millennia, one of the few ancient games we can still play by its original rules.

    Full history →
  3. c. 1400 BCE

    Morris

    Few games have been carved into as many different surfaces across as many centuries as Nine Men's Morris - from Egyptian temple roofs to English cathedral cloisters.

    Full history →
  4. c. 4th century BCE

    Go

    Go is one of the oldest board games still played in its original form - a game of territory and stones with roots that likely reach back over 2,500 years in China.

    Full history →
  5. Iron Age, c. 400 CE

    Hnefatafl (Viking Chess)

    One of the oldest board game families in Northern Europe, played across the Viking world for centuries before chess ever arrived there.

    Full history →
  6. 1732 CE (documented)

    Tablut

    The best-documented member of the entire Tafl family, thanks to one 18th-century naturalist's notebook.

    Full history →
  7. 1st millennium CE (debated)

    Mancala

    Mancala isn't one game but an entire family of them - among the oldest game families on Earth, built around the simple, universal act of moving seeds or stones between pits.

    Full history →
  8. traditional, exact origin undated

    Bagh-Chal

    Nepal's most iconic traditional board game: tigers hunting goats across a lattice of lines, with roots far older than any written record of the rules.

    Full history →
  9. traditional, exact origin undated

    Surakarta

    A traditional Indonesian board game named after the very city on Java where it is believed to have originated.

    Full history →
  10. c. 6th century CE

    Chess

    From an ancient Indian war game to the world's most studied board game - chess has crossed continents and centuries to become what it is today.

    Full history →
  11. c. 10th century CE

    Xiangqi

    Chinese chess shares deep roots with international chess, yet took its own distinct path over more than a thousand years to become the game played by hundreds of millions today.

    Full history →
  12. c. 12th century CE

    Checkers

    Checkers (draughts) is a game of simple rules and deep strategy, born from combining an ancient board with a newer way of capturing.

    Full history →
  13. 16th century CE

    Shogi

    Japan's take on the chess family, and the only major variant where a captured piece doesn't leave the game - it joins the side that captured it.

    Full history →
  14. c. 16th-17th century CE

    Fanorona

    The national board game of Madagascar, and a source of real cultural pride and legend.

    Full history →
  15. 17th century CE (name)

    Backgammon

    Backgammon's family tree runs through nearly every civilization that has rolled dice - from ancient Mesopotamia and Rome to the coffee houses of the modern Middle East.

    Full history →
  16. 1697

    Peg Solitaire

    A single-player puzzle old enough to have its own court legend, and the subject of one of the more surprising results in recreational mathematics.

    Full history →
  17. 19th century

    Klondike

    The single most iconically named, most widely played patience game there is - so ubiquitous that for a couple of generations of computer users, "Solitaire" meant this one specific game.

    Full history →
  18. 1880s

    Dots and Boxes

    A pencil-and-paper game more than a century old, and a genuine subject of serious mathematical study.

    Full history →
  19. 1883

    Reversi

    A Victorian parlor game, reinvented twice over a century apart.

    Full history →
  20. 1884

    Halma

    An American reworking of an older German game, popular for well over a century as a simple race that's easy to learn but still rewards careful planning.

    Full history →
  21. early 20th century

    Gomoku (Five in a Row)

    A simple line-them-up game whose very simplicity forced competitive players to change its rules.

    Full history →
  22. 1928

    Chinese Checkers

    A star-board descendant of the older game Halma, marketed under an exotic name that has nothing to do with where it was actually invented.

    Full history →
  23. 1942

    Hex

    A rare case of the same simple, elegant game being invented twice, independently, by two people who'd go on to become well known for entirely different reasons.

    Full history →
  24. 1956 (as Yahtzee)

    Yatzy

    A dice game whose scoring categories - pairs, three and four of a kind, a full house, a straight - read like a hand of poker, because that's exactly where they came from.

    Full history →
  25. 1966 (as Cross Sums)

    Kakuro

    Like Sudoku, Kakuro started life in an American puzzle magazine under a different name, and only became a household word decades later on the other side of the Pacific.

    Full history →
  26. 1970

    Mastermind

    A code-breaking game of pure logic, with no rival player to outwit, that grew out of a much older pencil-and-paper guessing game and went on to become a favorite subject for computer scientists studying how to search for a hidden answer efficiently.

    Full history →
  27. 1974

    Four in a Row

    A simple gravity-powered idea that turned into one of the best-selling games of the 20th century.

    Full history →
  28. 1978

    FreeCell

    A century-old card game family whose most famous variant, unusually, is famous largely because of a very specific piece of 1990s software.

    Full history →
  29. 1979

    Sudoku

    Unlike every other game in this collection, Sudoku has no ancient pedigree at all - it's barely older than the personal computer.

    Full history →
  30. 1985

    Nonograms (Picross)

    A logic puzzle invented independently, on opposite sides of the world, within a few years of each other - and named after one of its two inventors.

    Full history →
  31. 1986

    Mahjong Solitaire

    A single-player tile-matching game named after, and visually borrowing from, a completely different four-player game it has almost nothing else in common with.

    Full history →
  32. 1987

    Abalone

    A modern classic: one of the few abstract strategy games designed in the late 20th century to become a genuine, lasting commercial and competitive success.

    Full history →
  33. 1988

    Amazons

    The Game of the Amazons is a modern abstract strategy game about carving up territory by permanently blocking off the board, one square at a time.

    Full history →
  34. 1989

    Minesweeper

    A puzzle whose defining rule - a completely safe first move - only became standard once computers, rather than a printed page, could generate a fresh board on demand.

    Full history →
  35. 1995

    Lights Out

    A 1990s handheld electronic puzzle that turned out to hide a genuinely elegant piece of mathematics underneath its simple button grid.

    Full history →
  36. 1997

    Wall Maze

    PaperGames's Wall Maze is our version of Quoridor: a modern abstract strategy game that turns a simple race across a grid into a genuine battle of wits, just by adding a handful of walls.

    Full history →
  37. 1998 (Windows)

    Spider Solitaire

    A two-deck patience game with no exact birth certificate, like most of the family it belongs to - but one whose modern fame traces back to the very same company that made Klondike a household name.

    Full history →
  38. 2012 (Windows 8)

    Pyramid Solitaire

    A patience game built around a completely different mechanic from Klondike, FreeCell, or Spider: instead of building sequences, you clear the board by pairing cards that add up to 13.

    Full history →
  39. 2014

    Card Tactics

    A modern addition to the family of abstract strategy games, built around a small twist on chess-like movement: instead of every piece having a fixed way of moving, a shared deck of cards decides what's possible from turn to turn.

    Full history →
  40. 2014

    2048

    A game built almost entirely out of other games' ideas, made in a weekend, and cloned by more people than perhaps any browser game before it.

    Full history →
