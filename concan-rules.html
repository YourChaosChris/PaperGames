Concan Rules

How PaperGames plays Concan (also written Conquian). The game has come down in several versions that differ in details; the version played here is described below.

The basic idea

Cards and players
A pack of 40 cards: the usual pack without Eights, Nines and Tens, so Ace, 2 to 7, Jack, Queen and King in four suits. Two players get ten cards each, and one card is laid face up to start the discard pile.
Melds
A meld is a set of three or four cards of the same rank, or a run of three or more cards of one suit in order. The order is Ace, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, Jack, Queen, King - since Eight to Ten are missing, the Jack follows the Seven, so 7, Jack, Queen is a valid run. The Ace is always low.
Your turn
The top card of the discard pile may only be taken if it is laid out at once, in a new meld with cards from your hand or on one of your own melds. Otherwise draw the top card of the stock. Then you may lay out more melds or add cards to your own melds, and you end your turn by discarding one card.
The compulsory take
This is what makes Concan special: every discarded card is offered to the opponent, and if they can use it, they MUST take it and lay it out. The game checks this for you and tells you when a card has to be taken. So think twice before discarding a card your opponent could use.
Winning
The first player with eleven cards laid out wins at once. Since every turn ends with a discard, your last hand card can only be laid out when it makes eleven. If the stock runs out before anybody has eleven cards laid out, the game is a draw.
The version played here
Old descriptions of Concan differ, for example in whether a drawn card goes into the hand or is shown to both players, and in how many cards must be laid out. PaperGames plays the version described on this page: drawn cards go into the hand, every turn ends with a discard, a usable discard must be taken, and eleven laid-out cards win.
