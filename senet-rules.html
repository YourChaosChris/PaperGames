Senet Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Senet - a modern reconstruction of a game whose original rules were lost, so consider this one interpretation rather than the definitive one.

The basic idea

Pieces and the path
Each player has 5 pieces on a track of 30 squares, numbered in a boustrophedon path: left to right along the top row, right to left along the middle row, and left to right again along the bottom row. On your turn, throw the sticks and move one of your pieces forward by that many squares.
Throwing the sticks
Four two-sided throwing sticks stand in for dice: the throw is the number of sticks landing marked-side up, except throwing none of them up counts as the best result, 6, rather than 0. Throwing a 1, 4 or 6 grants another turn.
Stacking and captures
Two or more of your own pieces may share a square, which protects them - the opponent can't land there. A single piece standing alone can be captured: an opposing piece landing on it simply swaps places with it.
The three named houses
Square 15, the House of Rebirth, is always safe for whoever holds it. Square 26, the House of Beauty, can't be jumped over - a piece must land on it exactly before moving further. Square 27, the House of Water, is dangerous: a piece landing there is immediately washed back to the House of Rebirth (or square 1, if that's occupied).

How a game ends

Winning
A piece needs an exact throw to move off the end of the track. The first player to bear off all five of their pieces wins.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
