Rummy (Rommé) Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Rommé, the German rummy with two packs of cards and jokers.

The basic idea

The cards
110 cards: two packs of 52 French cards plus 6 jokers. 2 to 4 players. Black suits (spades, clubs) are drawn filled, red ones (hearts, diamonds) hollow; a joker shows a star.
Dealing
The dealer gets 14 cards, everyone else 13. No card is turned face up: the dealer begins and ends that first turn by discarding a card. The dealer changes from round to round; in the first round the setting "Who starts" decides.
A turn
Draw the top card of the stock or take the top card of the discard pile. Then you may lay out new melds and add cards to melds on the table. End your turn by discarding one card.
Melds
A set is three or four cards of the same rank in different suits - no suit twice, even though two packs are in play. A run is at least three cards of one suit in order.
The Ace
In a run the Ace goes after the King (Q-K-A) or before the Two (A-2-3), but never round the corner: K-A-2 is not allowed.
First meld
Your first meld must be worth at least 40 points, laid out in one turn - one or more melds together. Before it you may not add to melds on the table. The minimum can be set to 30, 40 or 51 points before the game starts. Here you lay the melds aside first ("staged"); "Lay out first meld" puts them on the table once they reach the minimum, "Take back" returns them to your hand.
Card values
Jack, Queen and King count 10, Two to Ten their number, the Ace 11 - only in a run A-2-3 in the first meld does it count 1. A joker counts like the card it stands for in the first meld, and 20 in your hand when the round is scored.
Adding to melds
After your first meld you may add cards to every meld on the table, whoever laid it out. That is the difference to Concan, where you may only add to your own melds.
Jokers
A joker stands for any card. If a joker lies in a meld and you hold the card it stands for, you may exchange them - but the joker must be laid out again at once in a new meld. Example: in Spade-6, Heart-6, Joker the joker stands for Club-6 or Diamond-6. PaperGames rule: only a player who has made the first meld may swap a joker.

End of a round

Going out
Whoever has no cards left ends the round - by laying out or by discarding the last card. Everyone else scores the cards left in their hand as penalty points; they add up from round to round.
When the stock runs out
PaperGames rule, not from the source: if the stock is used up, the discard pile except its top card is shuffled and becomes the new stock. This happens at most twice per round; when the stock runs out a third time, the round ends without a winner and everyone scores the cards in their hand.
Several rounds
Before the game you can set how many rounds are played: 1, 3, 5, 10, 20 or 25. The penalty points add up over all rounds; after the last round the player with the fewest penalty points wins the game, and if several share the fewest it is a draw.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time; against the computer that counts as a loss.

House rule: taking cards from melds

This is a house rule that does not occur in usual Rommé; it can be switched on before the game. After your first meld you may take a card from any meld on the table, also from your opponents', as long as at least three cards stay there as a valid meld: from a run only the first or the last card, from a set of four any one. Jokers cannot be taken - for them the swap applies. A taken card must be laid out in a new meld in the same turn; until then you may not discard. "Take back" puts the table and your hand back as they were before you took. You may take several cards in one turn.

A card you took may also be swapped for a joker on the table. The joker then has to go into a new meld in the same turn instead of the card - it may not be added to a meld, even when "add the swapped joker" is switched on. So the swap is only possible if you can lay out a new meld with the joker.

House rule: splitting runs

This house rule does not occur in usual Rommé either; it can be switched on before the game. After your first meld you may split a run on the table, yours or an opponent's, with a card from your hand that has the same rank and suit as a card in the run - the second pack makes that possible. The run becomes two: the first ends with the card that was there, the second starts with your new card. Both must have at least three cards afterwards. Example: 2 3 4 5 6 7 of Spades plus a 4 of Spades gives 2 3 4 and 4 5 6 7; with a 3 it would not work (2 3 and 3 4 5 6 7). Both runs belong to whoever owned the old one. Where a joker stands for the card, the run cannot be split there; jokers elsewhere stay where they are. Sets are never split. Like adding cards, you may split several times in one turn. To split, select the card in your hand and tap the run.

House rule: adding the swapped joker

This house rule can be switched on before the game as well. A joker you get by a swap must still go on the table in the same turn, but besides a new meld you may also add it to any meld on the table, yours or an opponent's, including the one it came from (for example at the end of the run). The swap is then allowed whenever the joker fits into a new meld or onto at least one meld on the table. Until the joker lies on the table you may not add other cards or discard. If your hand is empty after adding the joker, the round ends as usual. The joker cannot be used to split a run.

Variants

Variant "take the discard only to lay it out" (can be switched on before the game): the top card of the discard pile may only be taken if it is laid out in a meld in the same turn; before the first meld it is part of the first meld and counts towards the minimum. Until it lies on the table you may not discard. Source: Wikipedia (Deutsch), „Rommé“: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romm%C3%A9 (retrieved 6 October 2026). PaperGames rule, not from the source: if laying it out doesn't work, "Put back and draw from the stock" returns the card to the top of the discard pile, dissolves staged melds holding it, and you draw the top card of the stock instead.

Which version is played here

Rommé is played with many house rules. PaperGames follows the German Wikipedia article: 110 cards, 14 cards for the dealer, a first meld of at least 40 points (adjustable to 30 or 51), adding to all melds, joker swap with an immediate new meld. Two points are PaperGames rules and marked as such: the joker swap only after the first meld, and reshuffling the discard pile.
