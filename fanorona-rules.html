Fanorona Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Fanorona - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The board
45 points in a 5x9 grid, connected by horizontal, vertical, and some diagonal lines - the diagonals alternate direction from cell to cell, so about half the points ('strong' points) connect to all 8 neighbors while the rest ('weak' points) connect only orthogonally. Each player starts with 22 pieces filling their two nearest rows plus half of the middle row, alternating colors, with the very center point left empty. White moves first.
Capturing
Slide a piece along a line to an adjacent empty point. Depending on the direction you moved, this can capture a straight line of enemy pieces by approach, by withdrawal, or (if both are possible) your choice of either - see the examples below. Capturing is mandatory: if any capturing move is available anywhere on the board, you must play one instead of a quiet (paika) move.
Capture by approach
Move a piece so that the point immediately beyond your new position, in the same direction you just moved, holds an enemy piece. That piece, and every enemy piece directly behind it in an unbroken line, is captured.
Capture by withdrawal
Move a piece away from an enemy piece that was sitting right behind its old position, in the exact opposite direction of your move. That piece, and every enemy piece directly behind it in an unbroken line, is captured.

Continuing a capture
After capturing, the same piece may keep capturing again from its new position - but unlike checkers, you're never forced to: you can stop after any capture. Two extra rules apply while chaining: the piece can't slide in the exact same direction twice in a row, and it can never land on a point it has already visited earlier in the same turn.

How a game ends

Winning
You win by capturing every one of your opponent's pieces, or if your opponent has no legal move at all on their turn.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
