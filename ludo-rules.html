Ludo Rules

A quick reference for PaperGames's version of the classic cross-shaped race game. You play one color yourself; the other 2-3 colors are either built-in computer opponents or, in local hotseat, other players taking turns on the same device.

The basic idea

Tokens and the board
Each of the 2-4 colors has 4 tokens, starting in that color's home base. Tokens race around a shared 52-square outer track, then up their own color's private 5-square home stretch, into the center finish hub.
The die
A single six-sided die is rolled each turn. If no token can legally use the number rolled, the turn passes without moving.
Leaving home
A token can only leave its home base and enter the board on a roll of 6, moving onto that color's own start square.
Rolling a 6
Rolling a 6 grants an extra roll straight after moving (or after failing to find a legal move). Rolling three 6s in a row forfeits the turn instead, without moving, to stop a lucky streak going on forever.
Colors and shapes
On an e-ink screen the four colors look almost the same, so each color also has its own shape: red a filled circle, green an open circle, yellow a filled triangle and blue an open square. The shape is used everywhere - for the tokens, the waiting tokens in each home base and the finish count above the board. A black wedge in a square's corner, or a thick double frame around a waiting token, marks a token that may move with the current roll.

Capturing and safe squares

Capturing
Landing exactly on a square occupied by a single opponent token sends that token straight back to its owner's home base, to start over. A token already safely inside its own home stretch can never be captured.
Safe squares
Each color's own start square, plus one star-marked square further along the track, are safe: no capture can happen there, whoever is standing on it.
Blocks
Two of your own tokens sharing a square form a block. Opponents can neither land on nor move past a block, so it's a strong way to hold a stretch of track - though it also stops your own further tokens getting past it going the other way round.

How a game ends

Winning
The first color to bring all 4 of its tokens into the center finish hub wins immediately, ending the game. A token must land exactly on the finish - a roll that would overshoot it can't be used for that token, though another token might still have a legal move.
Resignation
Whoever's turn it is can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in favor of one of the remaining colors.
