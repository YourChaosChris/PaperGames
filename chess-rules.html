Chess Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays the game.

How the pieces move

♙ Pawn
One square forward (two from its starting square), captures one square diagonally forward. Reaching the far side promotes it to any other piece (PaperGames always promotes to a queen).
♘ Knight
Moves in an L-shape (two squares one way, one square perpendicular) and is the only piece that can jump over others.
♗ Bishop
Any number of squares diagonally. Stays on one square color for the whole game.
♖ Rook
Any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
♕ Queen
Any number of squares in any direction - combines the rook and bishop.
♔ King
One square in any direction. Can never move into check.

Special moves

Castling
The king moves two squares toward a rook, and that rook jumps to the square beside the king. Only if neither piece has moved yet, the squares between them are empty, and the king isn't in, through, or ending in check.
En passant
If an enemy pawn advances two squares and lands beside yours, your pawn may capture it as if it had only moved one square - but only immediately on the next move.
Promotion
A pawn reaching the last rank becomes another piece, almost always a queen.

How a game ends

Checkmate
The king is in check with no legal move to escape it. That side loses.
Stalemate
The side to move has no legal move and isn't in check. The game is a draw.

Draws by rule

Threefold repetition
The exact same position occurs three times.
Fifty-move rule
Fifty moves pass (by both sides) with no pawn move and no capture.
Insufficient material
Neither side has enough pieces left to possibly checkmate (e.g. king vs. king, or king and bishop vs. king).
