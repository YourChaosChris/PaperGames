Shogi Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Shogi (Japanese Chess) - useful if you're coming from international chess, since several ideas here work differently.

The basic idea

王 The board and pieces
Each side has 20 pieces on a 9x9 board: a King, a Rook, a Bishop, two Golds, two Silvers, two Knights, two Lances and nine Pawns. Pieces are plain pentagons pointing toward the opponent - which side a piece belongs to is which way it's facing, not its color. Black always moves first.
Promotion
Every piece except the King and Gold can promote to a stronger form when it moves into, out of, or within the farthest three rows of the board. Promotion is optional, except when a Pawn or Lance reaches the very last row, or a Knight reaches either of the last two rows - there it's forced, since the piece would otherwise never have another legal move.
Captures and drops
Capturing a piece doesn't remove it from the game: it switches sides, demotes back to its unpromoted form, and joins the capturing player's hand. On a later turn, instead of moving a piece already on the board, that player may drop a piece from their hand onto any empty square - one of Shogi's signature ideas, and the reason games rarely run out of material.
Drop restrictions
A Pawn or Lance can't be dropped onto the last row, and a Knight can't be dropped onto either of the last two rows, since they'd have no legal move there. A Pawn can't be dropped onto a file that already has one of your own unpromoted Pawns, and a Pawn drop may never deliver checkmate outright (it may still deliver check, just not a mate).

How a game ends

Winning
A King in check that has no way to escape, block, or capture the checking piece is checkmated - the game ends immediately.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
Repetition (sennichite)
If the same position - the same pieces on the same squares, the same pieces in both hands, and the same player to move - occurs four times, the game is a draw. Exception: if one player gave check with every one of their moves throughout the repetition, that player loses instead. PaperGames applies this rule automatically.
