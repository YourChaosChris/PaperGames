Alquerque Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Alquerque, the jumping game described in the Book of Games of Alfonso X of Castile.

The basic idea

The board
The board has 25 points in a 5x5 grid. Neighbouring points are joined across and down, and some of them also diagonally: diagonal lines run only through every other point, so that the four corners and the centre are joined in all eight directions. Pieces may only move along the lines that are drawn.
Setting up
Each side has 12 pieces: White (rings) at the bottom, Black (filled discs) at the top. Each side fills the two rows nearest to it and the two points of the middle row on its own right-hand side. The centre point stays empty. White moves first.
Moving
On your turn, move one of your pieces along a line to a neighbouring empty point. Pieces may move in any direction, also sideways and backwards.
Capturing
A piece captures by jumping along a line over a neighbouring enemy piece to the empty point directly behind it. The jumped piece is removed. If the same piece can then jump again, it keeps going in the same turn, even changing direction.
Capturing is compulsory
If you can capture, you must: while a capture is available, PaperGames only lets you make capturing moves, and a piece that can keep jumping has to go on until it can't. When several captures are possible, you may choose any of them, not only the longest.

How a game ends

Winning
You win when your opponent has no pieces left, or has pieces but no legal move on their turn.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favour. There is no automatic draw: if neither side can win any more, end the game by resigning or start a new one.

Which version is played here

Old descriptions of Alquerque leave some details open, and players have handled them differently. In some versions a player who misses a capture loses the piece that should have captured. PaperGames uses the simpler form: a non-capturing move simply isn't offered while a capture is available. Pieces may move in every direction, and White moves first.
