Lights Out Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Lights Out - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

Pressing a cell
The board is a 5x5 grid where each cell is either on (dark) or off (light). Pressing any cell toggles it and its orthogonal neighbors above, below, left and right - between one and four neighbors, depending on whether the pressed cell sits on an edge or in a corner. Diagonal neighbors are never affected.
A concrete example
Pressing the center cell of a 3x3 area toggles it and the four cells directly above, below, left and right of it - five cells in total flip between on and off, while the four diagonal corners of that same 3x3 area are left untouched.
Edges and corners
A cell on the outer edge of the board has only three neighbors instead of four, and a corner cell has only two - pressing either still toggles the pressed cell itself plus whichever neighbors actually exist, never wrapping around to the opposite side.

Finishing a puzzle

Solved
The puzzle is solved the instant every light on the board is off. Every puzzle PaperGames generates is scrambled by pressing a random, known sequence of buttons starting from an all-off board, which guarantees a solution always exists - unlike a completely random starting grid, which isn't always solvable at all.
Difficulty levels
Easy, Medium and Hard control how many random button-presses scramble the starting grid - more presses tends to produce a more tangled-looking starting position, though it isn't a strict guarantee that the puzzle takes more moves to solve.
