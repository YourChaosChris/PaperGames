Abalone Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Abalone - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The board
A hexagon of 61 cells, arranged in rows of 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5. Each player starts with 14 marbles: their two nearest rows fully filled, plus the middle 3 cells of the next row. Black moves first.
Moving a single marble
Select one marble and slide it into any adjacent empty cell.
Moving a line
Select 2 or 3 of your own marbles that sit in a straight, unbroken line, then move the whole line one step in any of the board's 6 directions. There are two kinds of line move, described below.
Broadside move
The line steps sideways, off its own axis. Every cell the line moves into must be empty - a broadside move can never push anything.
Inline move
The line moves along its own axis, in the direction either of its two ends is facing. If the cell straight ahead is empty, the whole line simply slides forward.
Sumito (the push)
If the cell straight ahead of an inline move holds enemy marbles, you may push them - but only if your line is strictly longer than the contiguous enemy line blocking it (3 marbles can push 1 or 2, 2 marbles can push 1; 1 marble can never push). There must also be room to push into: either an empty cell just past the enemy line, or the edge of the board. If the enemy line runs off the edge of the board, its last marble is eliminated - the way a player scores.

How a game ends

Winning
The first player to push 6 of the opponent's marbles off the board wins.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
