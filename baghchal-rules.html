Bagh-Chal Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Bagh-Chal ("Tigers and Goats") - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The board
25 points in a 5x5 grid, connected by horizontal, vertical, and some diagonal lines - the same alquerque-style lattice as Fanorona's board, just square: the four corners and the center are full 8-direction hubs, and diagonals fan out from there, giving the board its distinctive lattice of triangles. Four tigers (bagh) start on the four corners. The other 21 points start empty - the 20 goats (bakhera) aren't on the board yet.
Placement phase
Goats move first each turn: the goat player places one new goat on any empty point. Then the tiger player moves (see below) - tigers can already move and capture from turn one, even while goats are still being placed. This continues until all 20 goats are on the board.
Movement phase
Once every goat has been placed, both sides just move: each turn, slide one of your own pieces one step along a connecting line to an adjacent empty point.
Capturing
A tiger next to a goat, in a straight line along one of the board's real connections, may jump over it to the empty point immediately beyond, removing the goat. Capturing here is optional, not forced: a tiger with a capture available may play a quiet move instead if it prefers to. (Some traditional rule sets make capturing mandatory whenever possible - this is a documented, deliberate choice for the more common modern ruleset, not an oversight.) Goats never capture.

How a game ends

Tigers win
By capturing 5 goats in total - the standard threshold (some regional variants use a different number, but 5 is the most common).
Goats win
By trapping every one of the 4 tigers so that the tiger to move has no legal move at all - no quiet move and no capture, for any of its tigers.
Resignation
A player can resign at any time, ending the game immediately in the opponent's favor.
