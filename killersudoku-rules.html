Killer Sudoku Rules

A quick reference for how PaperGames plays Killer Sudoku - useful if you're rusty, or learning.

The basic idea

The grid
As in Sudoku, thick lines divide the 9x9 grid into nine 3x3 boxes. Dashed lines also divide it into cages of one to five cells. Few or no cells start filled in.
The goal
Fill every cell with a digit from 1 to 9 so that each row, each column and each 3x3 box contains every digit exactly once. Every puzzle PaperGames generates has exactly one solution.
Cages
The small number in a cage's top-left cell is its sum: the digits in the cage must add up to it. Unlike in Calcudoku, no digit may repeat inside a cage.
Entering numbers
Select a cell, then pick a number from the pad below the board (or type a digit on a keyboard). A digit shown white on black repeats in its row, column, box or cage, or completes a cage with the wrong sum.
Difficulty
Easy puzzles have mostly small cages and a few more given digits; Hard puzzles have larger cages and only the givens needed for a unique solution, often almost none.
Hint
The "Hint" button helps in three stages. The first press marks where the next step is. The second press gives the reason, that is, the rule that applies there. The third press carries out the step (enters the number or builds the bridge). The hint works on your current position: if a number or bridge is wrong somewhere, it tells you that first. It never guesses - if the only way on is trying things out, it says so honestly. Hints are unlimited. A puzzle solved with hints still counts as a win; the statistics also show how many wins were with hints.

Finishing a puzzle

Solved
The puzzle is solved the moment every cell is filled and no row, column, box or cage breaks a rule.
